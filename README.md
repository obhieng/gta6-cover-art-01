Vice Darkroom Cover Studio — turn any photo into a magazine-style Vice cover, and develop it like film: the Darkroom gives you a draggable tone curve (Luma, Red, Green, Blue — click to add a point, drag to move it, double-click to remove it), exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, highlights, shadows, sharpen and clarity sliders, plus per-colour HSL shifts — all live in the preview and baked into the PNG. Smart Suggest reads your photo's real pixels (brightness, contrast, warmth, dominant colour) and recommends the grade, layout and title that fit it best, as three one-click cards. The Diptych layout makes the cover itself a before/after — raw photo on the left, developed cover on the right, labelled BEFORE / AFTER. The Cross Process treatment develops the film in the wrong chemicals (blue shadows, warm highlights), and Golden Hour / Blue Hour presets apply a full darkroom recipe in one click. The Procedural Art Kit paints sunburst rays, palm and skyline silhouettes, a sun disc and a perspective grid floor straight onto the cover in code (no assets, no uploads), speech/thought bubbles add comic dialogue you can drag anywhere, and the Storyboard layout tells the story in 4 numbered panels. Projects save and reload as one JSON file (photos included) and autosave in the browser, so a cover is never lost. Series mode builds a main cover plus a square and a portrait variant from one theme — change the theme once and all three follow, then export the whole series as 3 PNGs in one click. The Photo Lab adds background blur, a spotlight, color-pop (one hue stays vivid, the rest drains away) and three vintage film presets (VHS 86, Polaroid 95, Chrome 99); the template gallery shows eight finished covers as painted thumbnails, so you see the recipe before you click it; and a 3-step guided wizard (Foto → Gaya → Export) walks new users through, with the full editor always one click away. The v5 grade Sunset Noir (dark + orange rim) joins 9 other grades, and the title engine keeps 7 styles including Chrome Gradient. Six one-click Looks apply a complete cover recipe (layout + grade + backdrop + title style + treatment); the Magazine layout is a full-bleed single photo under the masthead. Underneath it all, the v2 Ink engine still turns any panel into comic-book art (posterize + edge ink + halftone dots, level adjustable per panel), with 10 layouts, 10 sunset/neon grades, 3 built-in Vice City scene backdrops with blend modes, per-panel captions, duotone/threshold/solarize/cross-process treatments, light-leak + vignette, a before/after compare slider, Surprise me + 20-step undo, and one-click PNG export in 1080×1080, 1080×1350 and 1920×1080 — or all three sizes at once. Built by an AI agent for make.fun's “Make GTA 6 Cover Art” bounty, $250 in USDC.

## Run it

```sh
node serve.ts
```

Then open http://localhost:8080. Upload 1–3 of your own photos (they never leave your machine) or use the two bundled samples, follow the 3-step wizard or jump straight to the full editor, pick a template from the gallery or a Look, press “Analyze photo &amp; suggest” and apply the Smart Suggest card that fits, develop the photo in the Darkroom (drag the tone curve, tune the light, or hit the Golden Hour preset), try the Diptych layout to show the raw photo next to the developed cover, switch on the Procedural Art Kit (sunburst, palms, skyline, sun disc, grid floor) and add a speech bubble, tell a 4-beat story with the Storyboard layout, turn on Series mode to build the landscape + square + portrait set in one theme, tune the title (curved, 3D, gradient, sticker, glow, chrome), the Photo Lab (blur, spotlight, color-pop, vintage film), set the masthead and cover lines, write a caption for every panel, switch any panel to Ink for the comic-poster look, slide Before ⇄ After to check the grade, save the project as JSON (or let autosave keep it), and export the cover as a PNG in three post sizes — or the whole series at once with “Export series — 3 PNGs”.
