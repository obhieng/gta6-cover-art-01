Vice Print Cover Studio — turn any photo into a magazine-style Vice cover, now print-ready: export the finished cover at real print sizes — A4 portrait 2480×3508 (300 dpi), A3 landscape 4961×3508 (300 dpi) and a 24×36″ poster 2400×3600 (100 dpi), all PNG — stamp your own logo on it as a watermark (your own PNG, 5 positions, size + opacity, baked into every export), and keep every cover you make in the session gallery (thumbnail + name + time, click to load it back into the editor). The fifteenth template, Vice Print, shows the print axis in one click: Showcase layout, chrome Anton title, print marks on. Underneath it all, real 3D tilt: lean the whole finished cover in perspective (tilt X/Y ±20°, camera distance, layered drop shadow — the depth is baked into the PNG, not a CSS trick) and present it as a Framed Poster, a Phone Screen, or a night Billboard — three procedural mockup exports, drawn in code with no assets. The new Showcase layout is built for the tilt: one framed photo on a sunset stage with the big title in the band below it. The fourteenth template, Vice Showcase, shows the whole new axis in one click. Underneath it all, two real OFL display fonts (Anton + Archivo Black, vendored locally, no network) for the big poster lettering: pick the title face and the tagline face, pair either with all 7 title styles including Chrome Gradient, and try the Sunset Strip layout — one wide photo strip over a giant title on a sunset gradient band. The thirteenth template, Vice Fonts, shows that axis in one click. Underneath it all, the freeform editor: pick any layout, switch Freeform on, and drag every photo anywhere on the canvas — resize it from a corner handle, rotate it from the ring above it, bring it forward or send it back, with pink snap guides when it lines up with the canvas centre or edge — and drag the title block itself to any spot, tilted as you like; every move, resize and rotation bakes into the PNG. Behind the panels the canvas background can be a solid colour, a 2-stop gradient (from your custom palette in one click), or a faint dots or grid pattern, and Print marks add a bleed edge, crop marks and a safe-area box to the export, so the cover is print-ready. The twelfth template, Vice Freeform, shows that axis in one click. Underneath it all, Batch mode still builds 6 different covers from 6 photos at once — each photo is read from its own pixels (brightness, contrast, warmth, dominant colour), and its grade, layout, title and frame are picked for that photo, so no two covers in the batch look alike — then export all six as 1080×1080 PNGs in one click. Five procedural frames border any cover and bake into the PNG — Film Strip with sprocket holes, Polaroid with a caption foot, Ticket Stub with perforations and ADMIT ONE, Torn Edge with a jagged paper border, Rounded with cut corners — and Smart Crop finds each photo's focus point (brightest, busiest region) and steers the panel crop onto it. The eleventh template, Vice Batch, shows the batch axis in one click. Underneath it all, the Palette Builder extracts the 5 dominant colours from your photo's real pixels, you pick 3 stops, and the grade becomes a custom gradient in those colours — save up to 4 palettes of your own, or start from 3 presets (Vice Sunset, Neon Nights, Palm Noir). Pin photos to the Moodboard scrapbook with tape corners, gold pins and handwritten captions, remix the cover every day with Cover of the Day (one deterministic recipe per date), and export all four sizes on one contact sheet. The tenth template, Vice Palette, shows the whole new axis in one click. Underneath it all, the CRT/VHS glitch (chromatic RGB split, scanlines, tracking noise, glitch slices and an RGB fringe on the title — every effect with its own switch and strength, rendered into the pixels and baked into the PNG, with VHS Tracking, CRT Split and Full Glitch presets in one click), Auto-caption (reads your photo's real pixels and writes three ready titles with matching taglines — deterministic, so the same photo always suggests the same three — and one click applies title + tagline), and procedural stickers (star, lightning bolt, mini palm, sun, neon heart) peel-and-stick onto the cover with a die-cut white edge, draggable anywhere, up to 6 at once. Export adds a fourth size — 1080×1920 vertical story — alongside 1080×1080, 1080×1350 and 1920×1080, and export-all saves all four PNGs at once, or one contact sheet with all four on it. The Darkroom gives you a draggable tone curve (Luma, Red, Green, Blue — click to add a point, drag to move it, double-click to remove it), exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, highlights, shadows, sharpen and clarity sliders, plus per-colour HSL shifts — all live in the preview and baked into the PNG. Smart Suggest reads your photo's real pixels and recommends the grade, layout and title that fit it best, as three one-click cards. The Diptych layout makes the cover itself a before/after — raw photo on the left, developed cover on the right, labelled BEFORE / AFTER. The Cross Process treatment develops the film in the wrong chemicals (blue shadows, warm highlights), and Golden Hour / Blue Hour presets apply a full darkroom recipe in one click. The Procedural Art Kit paints sunburst rays, palm and skyline silhouettes, a sun disc and a perspective grid floor straight onto the cover in code (no assets, no uploads), speech/thought bubbles add comic dialogue you can drag anywhere, and the Storyboard layout tells the story in 4 numbered panels. Projects save and reload as one JSON file (photos included) and autosave in the browser, so a cover is never lost. Series mode builds a main cover plus a square and a portrait variant from one theme — change the theme once and all three follow, then export the whole series as 3 PNGs in one click. The Photo Lab adds background blur, a spotlight, color-pop (one hue stays vivid, the rest drains away) and three vintage film presets (VHS 86, Polaroid 95, Chrome 99); the template gallery shows thirteen finished covers as painted thumbnails, so you see the recipe before you click it; and a 3-step guided wizard (Foto → Gaya → Export) walks new users through, with the full editor always one click away. The v5 grade Sunset Noir (dark + orange rim) joins 9 other grades, and the title engine keeps 7 styles including Chrome Gradient. Six one-click Looks apply a complete cover recipe (layout + grade + backdrop + title style + treatment); the Magazine layout is a full-bleed single photo under the masthead. Underneath it all, the v2 Ink engine still turns any panel into comic-book art (posterize + edge ink + halftone dots, level adjustable per panel), with 13 layouts, 10 sunset/neon grades, 3 built-in Vice City scene backdrops with blend modes, per-panel captions, duotone/threshold/solarize/cross-process treatments, light-leak + vignette, a before/after compare slider, Surprise me + 20-step undo, and one-click PNG export in 1080×1080, 1080×1350, 1920×1080 and 1080×1920 — or all four sizes at once, or one contact sheet. ## Run it

```sh
node serve.ts
```

Then open http://localhost:8080. Upload 1–3 of your own photos (they never leave your machine) or use the two bundled samples, follow the 3-step wizard or jump straight to the full editor, pick a template from the gallery or a Look, export the cover at print sizes (A4 + A3 + Poster, one click) or stamp your own logo on it first (upload a PNG, pick a corner, size and opacity — it bakes into every PNG), save covers to the session gallery and click any saved cover to load it back, tilt the cover in 3D (sliders or one-click presets — Flat, Lean left/right, Pop-out, Showcase lean) and export it as a Framed Poster, Phone Screen or Billboard mockup, upload up to 6 photos in Batch mode and press “Build batch” for six different covers at once (or “Export batch” for all six 1080×1080 PNGs), pick a frame from the Frames pack (Film Strip, Polaroid, Ticket Stub, Torn Edge, Rounded) and press “Smart crop” to steer a panel onto its photo's focus point, press “Extract palette from photo” and apply the 3 stops it reads from your photo (or a preset), pin the photos to the Moodboard scrapbook, press “Cover of the Day” for today's remix, press “Analyze photo &amp; suggest” and apply the Smart Suggest card that fits, press “Suggest captions” and apply the title that fits, develop the photo in the Darkroom (drag the tone curve, tune the light, or hit the Golden Hour preset), turn on the Glitch effects (VHS Tracking, CRT Split or Full Glitch) and add a sticker or two, try the Diptych layout to show the raw photo next to the developed cover, switch on the Procedural Art Kit (sunburst, palms, skyline, sun disc, grid floor) and add a speech bubble, tell a 4-beat story with the Storyboard layout, turn on Series mode to build the landscape + square + portrait set in one theme, tune the title (curved, 3D, gradient, sticker, glow, chrome), the Photo Lab (blur, spotlight, color-pop, vintage film), set the masthead and cover lines, write a caption for every panel, switch any panel to Ink for the comic-poster look, slide Before ⇄ After to check the grade, save the project as JSON (or let autosave keep it), and export the cover as a PNG in four post sizes — or the whole series at once with “Export series — 3 PNGs” — or all four sizes on one contact sheet.
